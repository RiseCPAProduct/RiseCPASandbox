## 2. What We Currently Know

### 2.1 Finding Information

> User: "Where do I find this?"

- **Files are spread across multiple locations.**
  Staff may need to look across 3–4 different drives or SharePoint locations.
- **Finding a document can take 10–15 minutes.**
  Staff sometimes spend significant time searching for the right file.
- **Staff sometimes ask coworkers where a file is.**
  Finding information can depend on knowing who to ask.
- **Experienced staff rely on what they already know.**
  People who have been at Rise CPA longer may know where certain documents
  are located.
- **New employees may have more difficulty finding files.**
  They don't have the same knowledge of where documents are stored.
- **Commonly used documents are easier to find.**
  Staff become familiar with the location of documents they use frequently.
- **Less frequently used documents are harder to locate.**
  Older or less-used information can require more searching.
- **Some documents have unclear or inconsistent locations.**
  Shared resources may not have an obvious "home."
- **Staff currently navigate through folders to find information.**
  The existing structure can require drilling through multiple levels.
- **Some important documents are buried within existing structures.**
  Examples include policies, templates, scorecards, and other process
  documents.
- **Performance-review scorecards are a common search need.**
  These are particularly relevant during performance-review season.
- **The employee policy manual is another important search need.**
  Staff may need to locate specific policies when situations arise.

### 2.2 Organization

> User: "Where should this information belong?"

- **Different departments currently organize their files differently.**
  There isn't one consistent organizational structure across the company.
- **Departments have developed their own folder structures.**
  Teams have been organizing information based on their own needs.
- **The client wants a standardized structure.**
  Rise CPA wants departments to follow a more consistent approach.
- **The client wants to reduce ad hoc folder creation.**
  Staff should not have to create new folders wherever they think
  information belongs.
- **The current system contains older and obsolete information.**
  Some files and folders are no longer relevant but remain in the system.
- **Historical information can add clutter.**
  Older items can make it harder to identify what is currently useful.
- **"Published Documents" is intended for finalized information.**
  These documents are meant to be the reliable versions available to staff.
- **Departments are being considered as the main organizational structure.**
  Examples include HR, Resource Management, Marketing, Finance, and
  Accounting.
- **The client has suggested a common structure within departments.**
  For example:
  Calendar -> Budget -> Minutes -> Projects -> Published Documents
- **The client is open to improving the existing folder hierarchy.**
  They mentioned a year/month structure but are open to a better way of
  organizing information.
- **SharePoint is becoming part of the organization's file system.**
  Older drives are being transferred into SharePoint, creating an
  opportunity to establish a clearer structure.
- **The goal is for staff to know where information belongs.**
  The client wants users to have an expected location for documents instead
  of searching through multiple possible locations.

### 2.3 Access & Users

> User: "What information should I be able to see or edit?"

- **Different employees need access to different information.**
  Not everyone at Rise CPA needs access to every department's files.
- **Partners have the broadest access.**
  Partners need access to everything and have write access.
- **Managers and employees generally have read access to Published
  Documents.**
  Published information is intended to be accessible without everyone being
  able to edit it.
- **Some employees need access based on their role.**
  For example, Finance needs access to budgets, while Marketing needs access
  to marketing information.
- **Amy, the Manager of Operations, needs broad access.**
  Her role requires access across the organization.
- **Department-specific access is important.**
  HR, Marketing, Finance, Resource Management, etc. may have different
  access requirements.
- **Staff, managers, and interns are being considered as users.**
  The concept should account for different types of internal users.
- **New employees need to be able to access the same relevant information.**
  The system shouldn't depend entirely on an employee already knowing where
  things are.
- **Access needs to be controlled within the shared environment.**
  Some information should be available broadly while other information
  should be restricted.
- **The current system has different locations with different access
  levels.**
  Files are spread across drives and SharePoint locations with different
  permissions.

### 2.4 Documents & Maintenance

> User: "How do I know if this is the most up-to-date version?"

- **Rise CPA has many different types of internal documents.**
  These include policies, templates, budgets, reports, contracts,
  spreadsheets, and other resources.
- **HR has a particularly large volume of documents.**
  Examples include employee contracts and resumes.
- **Resource Management also contains a large amount of information.**
  Much of it consists of Excel analysis and reporting.
- **Finance contains many spreadsheets.**
  Financial information is an important part of the internal document
  system.
- **Some documents are intended to be shared as finalized versions.**
  These are placed under Published Documents.
- **Published Documents are meant to provide reliable information to staff.**
  Employees should be able to access finalized documents without changing
  them.
- **The current system contains outdated information.**
  Some files and folders are no longer relevant.
- **Obsolete information can remain alongside current information.**
  For example, older building information is still present.
- **The organization is moving older drives into SharePoint.**
  This creates a need to determine what information should be kept, moved,
  or removed.
- **The client wants to reduce unnecessary clutter.**
  Old or irrelevant material should not make current information harder to
  find.
- **Staff currently create folders as needed.**
  This can make the overall document system less consistent.
- **Document maintenance will need to happen over time.**
  The system needs to remain organized as new documents are created and old
  ones become outdated.
